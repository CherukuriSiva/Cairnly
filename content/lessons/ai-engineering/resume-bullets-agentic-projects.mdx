<WhatYouWillLearn>
- Turn an agent project into bullets that survive a recruiter's 6-second scan
- Lead with the architecture and the safety rails, not the framework
- Quantify impact even on a personal project
- Avoid the buzzword bullets that get you filtered out
</WhatYouWillLearn>

<WhyThisMatters>
Recruiters and ATS filters skim for concrete, credible signals. "Built an AI agent with LangChain" says nothing; "Built a ReAct agent with a tool registry, max-iteration safety cap, and reflection-based hallucination check" says you understand the system. The right bullet is the difference between an interview and the reject pile.
</WhyThisMatters>

<Body>
## The formula

`Built/Designed [architecture] with [distinctive components], achieving [quantified outcome]`

Lead with what you *engineered*, name the parts that prove depth (the safety rails especially), and end with a number — even a self-measured one.

## Strong vs weak

❌ "Used LangChain to make an AI agent."
✅ "Built a ReAct agent (controller + tool registry + memory + reflection) with a max-iteration cap and citation-grounded self-correction, reducing unsupported answers by ~40% on a 50-query eval."

❌ "Worked with RAG and vector databases."
✅ "Designed a multi-tenant RAG service with chunk-level RBAC and an audit trail, enforcing access control at retrieval so forbidden documents never reached the model."

## Quantify even without prod traffic

No users yet? Measure on your own eval set: "cut p95 latency 35% with a semantic cache," "raised refusal recall to 0.92 on a 40-item safety set." Self-measured numbers beat no numbers.

## Words that help vs hurt

- **Help:** ReAct, tool registry, reflection, RBAC, eval harness, F1, p95, max-iteration cap.
- **Hurt:** "cutting-edge," "leveraged AI," "synergy," and naming only a framework.
</Body>

<InterviewExplanation>
"On a resume I lead with the architecture and the rails, not the framework: 'Built a ReAct agent — controller, tool registry, memory, reflection — with a max-iteration cap and citation-grounded self-correction.' Then I attach a number, even a self-measured one from my eval set, like reducing unsupported answers 40% on a 50-query test. Naming the safety rails and showing a metric is what signals I actually built and evaluated the thing, rather than just wiring a library together."
</InterviewExplanation>
