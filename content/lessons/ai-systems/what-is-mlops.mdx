<Body>
## MLOps in one sentence

MLOps is the practice of taking models from notebook to production *and keeping them healthy there* — reproducibly, observably, and automatically. It's DevOps with two extra problems: **data** changes, and **model quality silently degrades** even when the code doesn't.

## The lifecycle

1. **Train** — produce a model from data, reproducibly.
2. **Track** — log params, metrics, and artifacts for every run (so you can compare and roll back).
3. **Register** — promote a chosen run's model into a registry with a version.
4. **Serve** — load the registered model behind an API.
5. **Monitor** — watch latency, errors, and *drift* (the data shifting under you).
6. **Retrain** — when drift or new data warrants, loop back to step 1.

## Why DevOps isn't enough

In DevOps, the same code gives the same behavior. In ML, the *code* can be frozen while accuracy quietly rots because real-world inputs drifted away from the training distribution. That extra failure mode — silent quality decay — is the whole reason MLOps exists.

## Where LLM engineers touch it

Even pure RAG apps need the serving, monitoring, cost-tracking, and fallback halves of MLOps. You may not train a model, but you'll absolutely operate one.
</Body>
