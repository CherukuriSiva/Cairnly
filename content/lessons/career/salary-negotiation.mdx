## Anatomy of an Indian tech offer (2026)

An offer letter in India typically includes:

| Component | What it means | Negotiable? |
|---|---|---|
| **Base salary** | Fixed monthly pay (the most reliable number) | Sometimes at startups, rarely at large companies for freshers |
| **Variable pay / performance bonus** | Paid quarterly or annually based on performance ratings | Rarely negotiable; ask what % of employees actually receive it |
| **ESOPs / RSUs** | Stock options (startups) or restricted stock units (public companies) | Sometimes — especially at startups |
| **Joining bonus** | One-time lump sum paid in the first month | Often negotiable, especially if you have competing offers |
| **Relocation allowance** | One-time payment for moving to the office city | Usually standardized, sometimes flexible |
| **Benefits** | Health insurance, meal cards, learning budgets | Not negotiable for freshers |

### How to read the CTC number

**CTC (Cost to Company) is NOT your in-hand salary.** A Rs 12 LPA CTC might mean Rs 75,000/month in-hand after deductions for PF, gratuity, insurance, and variable pay. Always ask for the in-hand monthly breakup.

**Red flags in offer letters:**
- Variable pay is more than 20% of CTC — this means your "guaranteed" income is lower than the CTC suggests
- ESOPs are listed at a "fair market value" with no vesting schedule
- "Performance bonus up to 200% of variable" — ask what the average payout has been historically

## 2026 salary ranges for Indian CS freshers

These are base salary ranges (not CTC). Actual numbers depend on location, company stage, and individual negotiation:

| Company tier | Examples | Base salary range (LPA) |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1: Big Tech India** | Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Apple India | Rs 18-30 LPA base |
| **Tier 2: Top product companies** | Flipkart, Razorpay, PhonePe, Zerodha, CRED | Rs 12-22 LPA base |
| **Tier 3: Growth-stage startups** | Series A-C startups with strong funding | Rs 8-15 LPA base + ESOPs |
| **Tier 4: Mid-size IT / consulting** | TCS Digital, Infosys Power Programmer, Wipro Elite | Rs 6-10 LPA base |
| **Tier 5: Early-stage startups** | Seed/pre-seed, small teams | Rs 5-9 LPA base + significant ESOPs |

**Note:** These ranges shift yearly. Check levels.fyi/india and Glassdoor India for current data. Campus placement offers at top IITs/NITs skew higher; the ranges above are broader market averages.

## The negotiation conversation

### When to negotiate

- **After you have a written offer** — never negotiate verbally before seeing the letter
- **Within the response window** — usually 1-2 weeks; ask for an extension if needed
- **When you have leverage** — a competing offer, a strong interview performance, or a specific skill they need

### The script

**Step 1: Express genuine enthusiasm (important!)**

> "Thank you for the offer — I'm very excited about the [team/role] and the work [Company] is doing on [specific thing]. I'd like to discuss the compensation package."

**Step 2: State your ask with a reason**

> "Based on my research and a competing offer I've received, I was hoping the base salary could be closer to [target number]. My work on [specific project/skill] is directly relevant to the problems your team is solving."

**Step 3: Be specific about what you want**

Do not say "Can you do better?" — say exactly what you want:
- "Could the base be increased to Rs X?"
- "Would a joining bonus of Rs X be possible?"
- "Could we discuss the ESOP grant size?"

**Step 4: Be ready for "no" — and have a fallback**

If they cannot move on base salary, ask about:
- Joining bonus (often easier to approve as a one-time cost)
- Team/project placement (working on the project you want is worth more than Rs 1-2 LPA)
- Earlier performance review (6 months instead of 12)
- Start date flexibility
- Remote work days

### What NOT to do

- **Do not lie about competing offers** — the Indian tech ecosystem is small; people talk
- **Do not negotiate aggressively for your first job** — a Rs 1-2 LPA difference matters less than landing at the right team
- **Do not accept on the spot** — always say "Thank you, I'd like to review the details and get back to you by [date]"
- **Do not ghost after accepting** — if you change your mind, inform the company immediately. Renaging damages your reputation.
- **Do not negotiate via email if you can call** — tone gets lost in email; a phone call is better for back-and-forth

## Understanding ESOPs at Indian startups

If you receive an ESOP offer:

**Ask these questions:**
1. What is the current valuation and the strike price of my options?
2. What is the vesting schedule? (Standard: 4 years with a 1-year cliff)
3. What is the total option pool and what percentage of the company does my grant represent?
4. What happens to my vested options if I leave?
5. Is there a buyback policy? (Some Indian startups like Razorpay and Zerodha have done ESOP buybacks)

**Reality check:** For most early-stage startups, ESOPs are worth zero until a liquidity event (IPO or acquisition). Do not treat ESOPs as guaranteed income. Calculate your livelihood based on base salary alone.
