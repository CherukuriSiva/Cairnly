## The two failure modes

### Failure mode 1: Spray-and-pray
Applying to 200 jobs on LinkedIn Easy Apply with the same generic resume. Result: 200 rejections and zero signal about what to improve.

### Failure mode 2: Paralysis
Spending weeks "perfecting" your resume and portfolio, never actually applying. Result: zero applications, zero feedback, zero interviews.

**The fix:** A weekly routine that forces both quality (targeted applications) and quantity (a minimum number per week).

## The weekly rhythm

### Monday: Research (1 hour)
- Identify 10-15 job listings that match your skills
- Save them in your tracker (see below)
- For each, note: company name, role, team, required skills, application deadline, and one person at the company you could contact

### Tuesday-Wednesday: Apply (2 hours total)
- Submit 5-8 targeted applications
- Tailor your resume header/summary to each role (not a full rewrite — adjust the top line and reorder projects)
- Write a 2-sentence cover note if the portal allows it

### Thursday: Outreach (1 hour)
- Send 3-5 cold emails or LinkedIn DMs to people at your target companies
- Follow up on messages sent last week (one follow-up per person, max)
- Engage with 2-3 posts from people at target companies (meaningful comments, not "Great post!")

### Friday: Skill + content (1 hour)
- Work on a project (keep GitHub active)
- Post one technical update on LinkedIn (a project milestone, a learning, a short write-up)
- Review any interview prep material for upcoming interviews

### Weekend: Interview prep (as needed)
- Practice 2-3 behavioral questions using STAR-Lite
- Do 1-2 coding problems on LeetCode/Codeforces
- Review any company-specific material for upcoming interviews

## The job application tracker

Use a spreadsheet (Google Sheets works fine). Here is the schema:

```
| Company | Role | Source | Date Applied | Status | Contact Person | Follow-up Date | Notes |
|---------|------|--------|-------------|--------|----------------|----------------|-------|
```

### Status values:
- **Saved** — found the listing, haven't applied yet
- **Applied** — application submitted
- **Referred** — someone referred you internally
- **Screen** — recruiter screen scheduled/completed
- **Technical** — technical round scheduled/completed
- **Behavioral** — behavioral round scheduled/completed
- **Offer** — received an offer
- **Rejected** — received a rejection
- **Ghosted** — no response after 3+ weeks and 1 follow-up

### Tracker rules:
1. Update the tracker the same day you apply or receive a response
2. Set a follow-up date for every application (2 weeks after applying)
3. Review the tracker every Monday during your Research hour
4. If an application has been in "Applied" status for 3 weeks with no response, move it to "Ghosted" and move on

## 30-day targets

| Week | Applications | Outreach messages | Interviews | Portfolio updates |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | 5-8 | 3-5 | 0 (pipeline is empty) | 1 GitHub commit |
| Week 2 | 5-8 | 3-5 | 0-1 (screens start) | 1 LinkedIn post |
| Week 3 | 5-8 | 3-5 | 1-3 (technical rounds) | 1 GitHub commit |
| Week 4 | 5-8 | 3-5 | 2-4 (deeper rounds) | Reflect + adjust |

**After 30 days:** review your pipeline. If you have 0 screens from 25+ applications, your resume needs work. If you get screens but fail technical rounds, increase coding practice. If you pass technical but fail behavioral, practice STAR-Lite. The tracker gives you data to diagnose the problem.

## Where to find jobs (India, 2026)

### High-signal sources:
- **Company career pages directly** — always check the source; job portals are often delayed
- **LinkedIn Jobs** — filter by date posted (last week), location, and experience level
- **AngelList / Wellfound** — best for startup roles
- **Instahyre** — recruiter-first platform; strong for product companies
- **Cutshort** — good for mid-stage startups
- **Twitter/X** — follow CTOs and engineering managers; many roles are posted there first

### Low-signal sources (use sparingly):
- **Naukri.com** — volume is high but signal-to-noise ratio is low; use only for service companies
- **Indeed** — mostly non-tech or service company listings
- **Random Telegram/WhatsApp "job groups"** — high noise, many scams

### Highest-signal source:
- **Direct referrals** — reach out to alumni, seniors, and connections at target companies. A referred application is 4-10x more likely to get a response.

## Dealing with rejection and silence

### The math of job searching

Expect a funnel like this:
- 30 applications → 8-10 recruiter screens → 4-5 technical interviews → 1-2 offers

This means 90-95% of your applications will result in rejection or silence. This is normal. It is not a reflection of your ability — it is the base rate of the process.

### When you get rejected:

1. **Ask for feedback** (politely): "Thank you for letting me know. If you're able to share any specific feedback on my interview, I'd really appreciate it for my improvement."
2. **Log it in your tracker** with any feedback received
3. **Move to the next application** — do not dwell

### When you get ghosted:

1. Send one follow-up after 2 weeks: "Hi [Name], following up on my application for [Role] submitted on [Date]. Still very interested — happy to provide any additional information."
2. If no response after the follow-up, mark as "Ghosted" and move on
3. Do not take it personally — companies ghost candidates constantly. It says nothing about you.

## The mental game

- **Set a daily time limit** — job searching for 8 hours a day leads to burnout. 1-2 focused hours is enough.
- **Track leading indicators, not just outcomes** — "I sent 5 targeted applications this week" is a success metric you control. "I got an interview" depends on factors you cannot control.
- **Batch rejections** — check email/portals twice a day, not every 30 minutes. Constant checking creates anxiety.
- **Maintain your routine** — keep coding, keep building. The worst thing you can do during a job search is stop developing skills.
