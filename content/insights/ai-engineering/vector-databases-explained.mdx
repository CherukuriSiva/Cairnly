<Body>
## What vector databases do

Embeddings turn text, images, or other data into high-dimensional vectors where "similar things are close together." A vector database stores those vectors and answers one core question fast: *which stored vectors are nearest to this query vector?* Because exact nearest-neighbor search is too slow at scale, these systems use approximate nearest-neighbor (ANN) indexes to trade a sliver of accuracy for large speedups. This similarity search is what powers retrieval in a RAG pipeline.

## The categories

The wide field organizes into a handful of types:

- **In-process library — FAISS.** A similarity-search library (not a server) optimized for dense vectors and nearest-neighbor search. Runs inside your app, zero infrastructure, free. Ideal for prototypes and learning.
- **Postgres extension — pgvector.** Adds vector similarity search directly to PostgreSQL. No new service to operate if you already run Postgres.
- **Self-hosted open-source engines — Milvus, Qdrant, Weaviate, Chroma.** Milvus targets billion-scale workloads; Qdrant focuses on filtering, payloads, and efficient retrieval; Weaviate adds hybrid search, modules, and scalable semantic indexing; Chroma is a lightweight embedding DB popular for local prototypes and RAG experiments.
- **Managed cloud services — Pinecone, and managed tiers of the above.** Pinecone is a fully managed vector DB built for fast similarity search and production retrieval. You trade some control for someone else operating it.
- **Vector features bolted onto existing systems — Redis, Elasticsearch/OpenSearch, MongoDB Atlas, and others.** If you already run one of these, its vector capability may be "good enough" without adding a new dependency.

## A selection guide

- **Student or prototype:** FAISS or Chroma — zero infra, free, fast to start.
- **Already on Postgres:** pgvector — keep your data in one place, no new service to run.
- **Self-hosted production:** Milvus or Weaviate (or Qdrant) — open-source engines built to scale.
- **Production on the cloud, minimal ops:** Pinecone or a managed Qdrant tier — managed and scalable.

The cleanest decision rule: start from your existing stack and operational appetite, not from a benchmark leaderboard.

## What to actually benchmark

Once you have two or three candidates, measure the things that decide real-world fit:

- **Query latency at your scale** — performance on a toy dataset tells you nothing about millions of vectors.
- **Filtering support** — can it filter by metadata (tenant, date, category) efficiently alongside the vector search?
- **Hybrid search** — does it combine keyword and semantic search, which often beats either alone?
- **Cost** — total cost of ownership, including operations for self-hosted options, not just sticker price.

Pick the simplest option that clears your bar on those four, and resist switching until a concrete limit forces you to.
